Three phases per round. Unsettled agents fan out one per port, come back carrying one bit — was the neighbour occupied — and then one settles while the rest move on together through a port a probe reported empty. When no empty port is left the group backtracks along the port it arrived by.

Also a DFS, but settled agents help. One whose node can spare it vacates and travels with the group as a scout, so several ports are probed in the same round. Scouts later retrace the tree in post-order and drop back onto the nodes they own.

A DFS that builds a port-one tree: every vertex ends up with an incident tree edge carrying port 1 at one of its ends. Two things carry the O(k) bound — the search stops at the first batch that finds somewhere to go, and the traversal stops at dispersion rather than finishing the tree.

The rooted HEO-DFS of Sudo et al. The largest ID leads a DFS and the smallest explorer settles at each new node. Before each move, Probe() sends the agents at the node out one port each; settlers they find come back as helpers and probe too, so the probers double each pass, and the helpers walk home before the group moves. Every box is one synchronous step or none.

The general HEO-DFS of Sudo et al. with the Zombie method. Agents start on any nodes; each group, labelled (level, leader), runs its own HEO-DFS. Time runs in units of twelve slots, one step each: leaders meet and the weaker become zombies, settle and level up, probe one pass with helpers walking over, zombies chase the stronger group, and leaders move. legitimate() is checked before every step, and the run ends at the first legitimate configuration.
